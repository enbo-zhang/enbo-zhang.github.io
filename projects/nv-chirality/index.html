[P-01] Chirality-resolved quantum sensing

Seeing the handedness of spin waves.

A single quantum spin in diamond can tell which way a magnetic wave is turning — and so which way it is going.

Chapter 1

A quantum sensor, inside a diamond.

One missing atom turns a crystal into a compass for microwaves.

How the NV centre senses handedness

Nitrogen + vacancy

A flaw, on purpose.

Replace one carbon with nitrogen, leave its neighbour empty, and the diamond holds a single quantum spin: the NV centre.

Light in, light out

Green in. Red out.

A green laser makes the NV centre glow red. How brightly it glows reveals the state of its spin.

Same B · same frequency

Same frequency, opposite hands.

The same NV centre, shown twice: same magnetic field B, same microwave frequency, tuned to the |0⟩ → |+1⟩ line. Only the handedness of the field differs: σ− in one view, σ+ in the other.

Tuned to |0⟩ → |+1⟩

One stays bright. One dims.

The left-handed (σ−) field cannot drive |0⟩ → |+1⟩, so its glow stays bright. The right-handed (σ+) field can, and the glow dims.

Tuned to |0⟩ → |−1⟩

Retune, and the roles swap.

Tune the frequency to the |0⟩ → |−1⟩ line instead. Now the σ− field dims the glow and the σ+ field does nothing. The NV centre is a built-in chirality analyser.

Chapter 2

Spin waves have a handedness.

Ripples of magnetism carry information without moving any charge.

How spin waves carry a handedness

The stage

A magnetic film, and an antenna.

A thin film of yttrium iron garnet (YIG) sits under a gold stripline. A magnetic field, B, points along the stripline.

Launch

Pulse the antenna. Waves go both ways.

The magnetization starts to precess, and the precession travels outward as a spin wave.

Above the film

Each direction turns its own way.

Above the film, each wave's magnetic field rotates. Waves heading left turn right-handed (σ+); waves heading right turn left-handed (σ−).

Side view

Direction, written as rotation.

Look along B and it's plain: the sense of rotation encodes where the wave is going.

Chapter 3

Reading direction at a single point.

Put the sensor on a scanning tip, hover it over the waves, and watch which colour goes dark.

Detecting spin-wave direction with a scanning NV probe

The probe

A quantum spin on a scanning tip.

The NV centre sits at the apex of a diamond probe, hovering just above the film.

Scan

Left of the antenna: σ+. Right: σ−.

Fix the microwave on the σ+ line and scan across the stripline: the NV dims on the left and stays bright on the right. Reverse B, and the two sides swap.

Reflection

A mirror flips the hand.

An etched pit reflects the wave back at the same wavelength, and the two interfere. Reversing direction reverses handedness: on the σ+ line the NV reads the incident wave; re-tuned to the σ− line, it reads the reflected one.

Two images

Two waves, one point, two colours.

Tune the microwave to one line or the other, and image each wave on its own.

Both waves, two colours.

The instrument

I built the microscope.

A scanning NV-centre microscope, assembled from components and tuned by hand — the tool behind the data that follows.

A 3D model of the instrument, from the laser to the probe tip

The optics

Green light, built from parts.

A 532 nm laser, pulsed by an acousto-optic modulator and carried by fibre to the microscope table. A dichroic, a galvo scanner and relay lenses steer it; the red glow comes back the same way to a single-photon counter.

Down to the sample

Follow the beam.

A periscope drops the beam into the objective of the AFM. Underneath, a stick-slip coarse stage and a closed-loop piezo scanner raster the sample, nanometre by nanometre.

The probe

A diamond on a tuning fork.

The NV probe hangs from the top on a quartz tuning fork, which holds the diamond tip nanometres above the film. A waveguide probe lands on the stripline to launch the spin waves.

At the tip

One spin, reading the waves.

A single NV centre at the apex, above the YIG film. With the microwave on the σ+ line it dims over the left-going wave and stays bright over the σ− one.
