[02] About

I like building the instrument as much as the result.

I'm Enbo Zhang. My work sits where quantum physics meets hardware: I design and build measurement systems, then use them to answer questions about magnetism.

I'm a staff engineer at Western Digital, in HAMR characterization. As the DOE characterization owner for the reader and writer, I design the experiments that link head design and materials to recording performance, and build the analysis behind those decisions: response surfaces, micromagnetic modelling and root-cause analysis. That work feeds the qualification of Western Digital's first generation of HAMR drives.

I also lead an AI for manufacturing project: AI-in-the-loop R&D for the recording-head organisation. I'm building a knowledge base with data and metadata standards that AI can read, and packaging our retrieval, screening, analysis and simulation methods as specs that coding agents implement and run. The aim is to hand routine DOE design and analysis to agents, so engineers can spend their time on the physics.

Before that, I did my PhD in Electrical & Computer Engineering at Carnegie Mellon University, advised by Prof. Jian-Gang Zhu. There I built a scanning NV-centre microscope from components — atomic-force positioning, confocal optics, single-photon detection and microwave control — and with it we demonstrated the first polarization-selective quantum sensing, imaging spin waves by their handedness.
